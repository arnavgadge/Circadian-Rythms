> **Global disclaimer, repeated throughout this document as required:**
> CircadianIQ computationally *estimates* circadian phase (DLMO) from
> behavioral and environmental proxies. It has **never** been
> validated against real human DLMO measurements. Every dataset,
> metric, and diagnostic result in this document and in the underlying
> codebase is computed on **synthetic data** generated by CircadianIQ's
> own generative model. No result here is a clinical or medical claim,
> and CircadianIQ is not a diagnostic or treatment device.

## 1. Problem Definition

Circadian phase -- most precisely operationalized in chronobiology as
Dim Light Melatonin Onset (DLMO), the time at which endogenous
melatonin secretion begins under dim-light conditions -- is a
biological marker with substantial relevance to sleep timing,
alertness, and behavioral scheduling. Direct laboratory DLMO
measurement is expensive, intrusive, and impractical for continuous or
large-scale monitoring. CircadianIQ addresses the practical question:
**can a participant's circadian phase be usefully estimated from
routinely observable behavioral and environmental signals (sleep
timing, light exposure, screen use, meal timing, exercise, caffeine),
without laboratory measurement?** This is treated throughout the
system as an estimation problem, never as a substitute for, or claim
of equivalence to, an actual clinical measurement.

## 2. System Objective

CircadianIQ's objective is threefold: (1) produce a daily,
behaviorally-derived circadian phase estimate for a participant; (2)
translate that estimate, together with the participant's actual
behavior, into an interpretable alignment score describing how well
current routine matches the participant's own estimated biology; and
(3) close the loop with actionable, deterministic, non-medical
behavioral recommendations, whose real-world effectiveness is measured
observationally over time and used to personalize future
recommendations. A safety layer sits above all of this, gating what is
shown and never altering the underlying computation. The system is
explicitly a research/demonstration architecture, not a certified
health product.

## 3. Circadian Modeling Concept

CircadianIQ represents circadian phase with three distinct DLMO
concepts, layered by timescale:

- **Intrinsic DLMO** -- a participant's stable biological
  predisposition, set once (at onboarding, associated with a
  chronotype category) and never modified thereafter.
- **Adapted DLMO** -- a slowly-changing behavioral equilibrium, pulled
  a small fraction toward the direction implied by recent behavior each
  day, and a smaller fraction back toward intrinsic, bounded within a
  fixed offset of intrinsic.
- **Current DLMO** -- the fast, day-to-day dynamic estimate, moved by
  each day's behavioral effect and pulled toward the adapted value,
  bounded within a (larger) fixed offset of intrinsic.

This three-layer structure is used identically by two independent
subsystems: the Phase 3 synthetic data generator (which produces
ground-truth trajectories for model training) and the Phase 6
production state engine (which estimates `current_DLMO` from real
behavioral history with no XGBoost dependency). Every layer's numeric
parameters (adaptation/pull rates, bounds) are CircadianIQ's own
synthetic modeling framework -- documented as such, not claimed to be a
validated physiological model of human circadian regulation.

## 4. Synthetic Data Generation

Because no real behavioral/DLMO paired dataset is available to this
project, CircadianIQ generates a synthetic but biologically-motivated
longitudinal dataset: 1,000 participants x 30 days (30,000
participant-day records), yielding 24,000 six-day-window-to-next-day
sliding-window records. Generation proceeds in three stages: (1) a
one-time participant generation step assigning chronotype (Early 27% /
Intermediate 59% / Late 14%, exact stratified sampling), a
chronotype-conditioned MEQ score, and stable behavioral-personality and
sensitivity traits; (2) daily behavior generation from personality
baseline + weekday/weekend effect + stochastic noise + occasional
disruption events, deliberately generated **independently of the
evolving DLMO state** to avoid a circular `DLMO -> behavior -> DLMO`
loop that would make later supervised learning trivial; and (3) a
sequential day-by-day application of the three-layer state model
(§3, §6) driven by that independently-generated behavior.

Every numeric constant in the generator is explicitly labeled either
**source-backed** (cited from `CircadianIQ_Project_Document.docx`
section 5, itself citing published circadian-science literature -- see
`docs/synthetic_data_generation.md` §11 for the specific citations) or
**synthetic** (a project modeling choice with no literature source).
Reproducibility is guaranteed by subsystem-separated, integer-derived
random streams (never Python's randomized `hash()`), verified by
dedicated regeneration tests; a full generation run is reproducible
byte-for-byte given the same seed, participant count, and day count.

## 5. Participant Modeling

Each synthetic participant is characterized by: a fixed chronotype
category and intrinsic DLMO (drawn once from a chronotype-conditioned
normal distribution); a fixed MEQ (Morningness-Eveningness
Questionnaire) score, clipped to the real instrument's [16, 86] range
and categorized per the standard Horne & Ostberg (1976) bands; a
behavioral personality drawn independently of chronotype from seven
synthetic archetypes (consistent early bird, consistent night owl,
social jetlag student, chaotic sleeper, healthy stable routine,
screen-heavy user, late eater), each with its own synthetic
distribution of sleep/light/screen/meal timing tendencies expressed as
offsets from the participant's own stable baseline sleep-onset trait;
and three behavioral-sensitivity multipliers governing how strongly
that participant's DLMO responds to light, screen, and meal timing.
Chronotype and personality are intentionally allowed to combine freely
(e.g. an Early-chronotype "night owl" personality is possible),
representing behaviorally-driven misalignment against one's own
biological tendency -- a structural modeling choice, not a published
coefficient.

## 6. Stateful DLMO Simulation

For each simulated day, in strict date order, the three-layer state
model (§3) is applied via a single `step()` transition: (1) compute
that day's total behavioral effect in minutes (positive = delay,
negative = advance) from morning light, evening screen, last meal,
exercise, and caffeine timing, scaled by the participant's sensitivity
multipliers and clipped to a synthetic stability bound of [-90, +60]
minutes; (2) update `current_DLMO` using that effect plus a pull
toward `adapted_DLMO` plus (optionally) state noise, clipped to stay
within a fixed offset of intrinsic; (3) update `adapted_DLMO` more
slowly, pulled partly toward the day's behavioral direction and partly
back toward intrinsic. Day N's state is computed strictly from Day
N-1's state -- never recomputed from a fresh window -- which is
verified directly by a dedicated test proving identical behavior
produces different results depending on the prior state. Three
documented interaction effects (no-morning-light amplifying the
evening-screen delay; high morning light dampening the screen effect;
a very late meal being amplified for a "late habitual sleeper") are
applied via smooth, saturating response curves rather than discrete
lookup tables. All rates, bounds, and interaction multipliers are
explicit synthetic assumptions, centralized in one configuration
object and never scattered as bare literals.

## 7. Temporal Feature Engineering

The feature-engineering layer (Phase 4) converts exactly six previous
*complete* calendar days of a participant's behavior into a 68-feature
vector predicting the next day's `current_DLMO`. Feature groups: 48
raw daily circular-time signals (4 non-sleep clock-time signals --
evening screen end, last meal, morning light start, exercise time --
across 6 days, each encoded as sin/cos), 2 MSFsc features (circular
mean of window free-day sleep midpoints), 2 MEQ-derived features
(normalized MEQ, MEQ/MSFsc divergence), 10 flat window aggregates
(mean/std/ratio statistics), and 6 recency-weighted counterparts of a
subset of those aggregates (linear weights 1..6, oldest to newest,
renormalized over missing days). A three-tier missing-data fallback
chain (window -> personal history -> population default) resolves
every feature independently, with full provenance
(`missing_features`/`imputed_features`/`imputation_methods`) attached
to every generated vector; on the Phase 3 dataset, which has no
missing values by construction, every window achieves
`completeness == 1.0`.

**Leakage prevention** is enforced structurally, by explicit assertion,
and by automated validation: the feature window for target day N is
always days N-6..N-1, and no code path can read day N's own
observation into its own feature vector -- re-checked by 15 automated
sanity checks on every generated dataset.

## 8. Circular Time Representation

Every clock-time-valued signal in CircadianIQ (feature encoding, DLMO
state transitions, alignment scoring, fusion, safety pattern
detection, and effectiveness estimation) is represented and
manipulated using circular arithmetic, never bare linear hour
subtraction. The canonical encoding is `(sin(2*pi*h/24),
cos(2*pi*h/24))` for a clock hour `h` (`app.features.time_encoding`),
reused unmodified by every downstream phase that needs it (XGBoost's
target encoding, DLMO fusion's weighted-vector combination). Circular
*differences* use the shortest signed arc (`circular_diff_hours`):
23:50 to 00:10 is correctly computed as a 20-minute difference, never
the ~23h40m a naive subtraction would produce -- verified directly by
dedicated tests at every layer that performs a time comparison,
including explicit midnight-crossing worked examples in the alignment,
fusion, and recommendation-targeting documentation. This single,
consistently-applied convention is one of the system's most heavily
cross-checked structural guarantees (re-verified by Phase 17's
audit-wide midnight-crossing stress tests).

## 9. XGBoost Prediction

The Phase 5 model predicts `current_DLMO` as a regression over a
circular target, solved as two independent `xgboost.XGBRegressor`
models -- one for `sin(DLMO)`, one for `cos(DLMO)` -- each trained on
the identical 68-feature Phase 4 manifest, with predictions decoded
back to a clock hour via unit-circle normalization
(`atan2`-based decoding, insensitive to prediction-vector magnitude).
Hyperparameters (500 estimators, max depth 6, learning rate 0.05, 0.8
subsample/colsample, `objective=reg:squarederror`) are the project's
documented engineering starting point -- no hyperparameter search was
performed. Two independent single-output models were chosen over
native multi-output XGBoost for interpretability of per-target feature
importance/SHAP analysis, despite native multi-output support existing
in the installed XGBoost version.

## 10. Subject-Held-Out Validation

Model evaluation strictly separates participants, never rows:
`split_participants` shuffles participant IDs with a seeded RNG (seed
2025) into an 800-participant training set (19,200 windows) and a
200-participant held-out test set (4,800 windows), verified disjoint.
Within the 800 training participants, 5-fold `GroupKFold`
cross-validation (grouped by participant, never ordinary row-based
`KFold`) is used for stability assessment; the held-out test set is
touched exactly once, after final training on all 800 training
participants, and never during cross-validation or any hyperparameter
selection. All error metrics use circular clock-time differences
(`circular_error_minutes`), computed on decoded hours, never on raw
sin/cos component error.

**Held-out synthetic test results** (200 participants, 4,800 windows,
computed exactly once): MAE 24.47 min, RMSE 31.86 min, median absolute
error 19.71 min, 93.73% of held-out synthetic test windows were within
±60 minutes, signed mean error (bias) -0.82 min. **5-fold
cross-validation** (within the 800 training participants): MAE
25.22 ± 1.20 min, RMSE 32.50 ± 1.35 min, 93.03% ± 0.74% within ±60
minutes. Two non-learned baselines, computed on the identical held-out
test set, contextualize this result: a participant-mean baseline (MAE
57.2 min, 67.6% within ±60 min) and an MSFsc-proxy baseline (MAE 342.1
min, 0.0% within ±60 min, reflecting the structural ~5.5-6 hour gap
between sleep midpoint and DLMO that a non-learned proxy cannot
correct). These figures describe performance against Phase 3's own
synthetic generative model and must never be read or repeated as a
claim about real-human accuracy: the generator and the predictor share
common synthetic-world assumptions, so strong synthetic performance is
not independent evidence of real-world validity.

## 11. Stateful Circadian Estimator

Independently of the learned XGBoost model, the Phase 6 state engine
(§6) provides a second, mechanistic `current_DLMO` estimate computed
purely from behavioral history and state continuity, with a verified
structural guarantee of having **no dependency on the trained XGBoost
model or any `app.models` import**. This engine is the same
implementation used by the Phase 3 synthetic generator (a
backward-compatible translation layer, not a duplicated formula), so
the state-model estimator and the synthetic ground-truth trajectory
share identical transition mathematics -- a fact material to
interpreting the fusion diagnostic in §12.

## 12. DLMO Fusion

The Phase 7 fusion layer combines the XGBoost estimate and the state
estimate into one fused DLMO through a locked four-layer model: (1)
base weights determined by a data-quality tier (HIGH 0.65/0.35 down to
VERY_LOW 0.20/0.80, XGBoost/state respectively); (2) a
disagreement-based weight adjustment across four regimes (NORMAL /
MODERATE / LARGE / EXTREME, by circular minutes of disagreement); (3)
a biological continuity constraint capping the day-over-day change
from the *previous fused* value (never from either estimator alone);
and (4) a confidence penalty applied per disagreement regime. Fusion
itself never averages clock hours directly -- it combines weighted
unit-circle vectors and decodes the result, with a documented
non-random fallback for the (structurally rare) zero-magnitude edge
case. An explicit no-prediction rule can override a mathematically
computed value when upstream data quality (missing critical signals,
low window completeness, or low final confidence) does not support a
user-facing prediction.

A diagnostic run over the Phase 5 held-out test set showed the fused
estimate's MAE (12.89 min) improving substantially on the XGBoost-only
MAE (24.47 min). **This improvement must be read with an important
caveat**: in this synthetic dataset, the "state estimate" is produced
by replaying the exact same state-transition model that generated the
ground-truth target in the first place -- so, for this dataset, the
state estimate effectively *is* the answer key. The diagnostic
therefore demonstrates that the fusion mechanics work as designed, not
that fusion improves accuracy against independently measured, real
human DLMO. Every fusion parameter (weights, thresholds, caps,
penalties) is a locked project design choice, not a fitted or
biologically validated constant; the layer is not a Kalman filter and
carries no optimality claim.

## 13. Alignment Scoring

The Phase 8 alignment engine converts a fused DLMO and a participant's
actual behavior into a 0-100 alignment score,
`0.60 * phase_alignment + 0.40 * behavioral_alignment` (a locked
project weighting, not a scientifically validated ratio). Phase
alignment compares the most recently completed night's sleep onset
against an "ideal" sleep onset (`fused DLMO + 2.0h`, itself a project
modeling assumption) via a circular exponential-decay score curve.
Behavioral alignment averages five weighted six-day-window components
-- sleep consistency (25%), morning light (25%), evening screen (20%),
meal timing (15%), social jetlag (15%) -- reusing the same tier
boundaries the DLMO effect model already established, so alignment and
the underlying effect model can never silently disagree about what
counts as, e.g., heavy screen use. MEQ is structurally excluded from
alignment (verified by a dedicated source-scanning test) since it is
an ML feature, not a behavioral-alignment input. A score of 100 means
strong alignment with *that participant's own* estimated phase, not
conformance to any universal schedule.

## 14. Contributor Identification

The Phase 9 contributor-ranking engine identifies which of five
tracked behavioral signals (evening screen, morning light, meal
timing, social jetlag, exercise) most contributes to a participant's
*current* misalignment: `contributor_score = 0.60 *
normalized_phase_effect + 0.40 * alignment_deficit`, reusing the Phase
6 behavioral-effect model and Phase 8's alignment components directly
rather than recomputing either. Two signals have a documented
"mirror-image asymmetry": exercise has a phase effect but no alignment
component (phase-effect-only scoring), while social jetlag has an
alignment component but no established direct phase-shift formula
(deficit-only scoring); neither is ever assigned a fabricated value
for its missing side. Direction is classified relative to the
participant's own current phase deviation (`hurting` /
`opportunity_missed` / `neutral`), and a significance threshold
prevents a weak, mostly-noise signal from appearing as a major
contributor. A separate, robust median/MAD-based "today's anomaly"
detector flags unusual single-day values without ever altering the
six-day habit-level ranking itself (verified by a dedicated test
holding the ranking fixed while varying only the anomaly input). All
output language is strictly "modeled influence" / "estimated phase
effect" / "alignment deficit" -- never a causal claim.

## 15. Recommendation Engine

The Phase 10 recommendation engine deterministically converts ranked
contributors into exactly one primary intervention plus up to two
supporting nudges (never three equal-priority suggestions), drawn
exclusively from a database catalogue of six behavioral interventions
(morning light, evening screen reduction, consistent sleep schedule,
earlier last meal, exercise timing, reduced late caffeine) -- no LLM,
no free-form text generation. Selection combines contributor
relevance, expected impact, and feasibility, weighted by a
misalignment-severity band (`good`/`mild`/`moderate`/`severe`, from
Phase 8's alignment score). A locked "graduated target" rule never
asks for the theoretically ideal value immediately -- it targets
roughly halfway to it, using the same circular-time utilities so a
target crossing midnight is computed as the short way around. A
cooldown mechanism favors continuity of an already-active primary
recommendation over needless churn. Every catalogue numeric value
(including `literature_expected_improvement_minutes`) is an explicitly
labeled project design placeholder, not a verified literature-cited
effect size. Recommendations are strictly behavioral (screen, light,
meal, exercise, caffeine timing) -- never medication, melatonin
dosing, or diagnosis-based treatment.

## 16. Intervention Feedback

The Phase 12 effectiveness system measures whether a recommended
intervention appears **associated with** improved alignment, through a
three-phase progression of increasingly rigorous, but still
non-causal, observational analysis: (1) `observational` -- a
compliance-adjusted before/after comparison of a 7-day baseline versus
the most recent 7 days of an intervention episode; (2)
`matched_observational` -- a within-person comparison against the
participant's own most-similar prior period on all *non-target*
signals; (3) `regression_controlled_estimate` -- a personal linear
regression of daily DLMO change on compliance degree plus behavioral
covariates, requiring at least 21 complete observations. The
dispatcher always evaluates Phase 1 first and reports the highest
phase whose own sufficiency gate independently clears -- never a
stronger-sounding phase reached prematurely. Compliance is scored via
an asymmetric directional-decay function (full compliance at or past
the target in the intervention's own desired direction; decay only for
shortfall). Confound detection flags, but does not causally adjust
for, other behaviors that changed substantially during the same
period. User-facing labels are fixed and phase-appropriate ("Observed
change" / "Change compared with a similar previous period" /
"Estimated personal effect") -- never "caused."

## 17. Personalization

The Phase 13 personalization engine graduates each
(participant, intervention-type) pair independently through
`literature -> personal_ranking -> personal_effect -> adaptive_policy`,
gated purely by accumulated observation count and weeks-active
thresholds (personal_ranking: 3 obs / 3 weeks; personal_effect: 14
obs / 6 weeks; adaptive_policy: 42 obs / 12 weeks -- all documented
project engineering graduation rules, not statistical or clinical
requirements). Personal ranking combines effect magnitude and
compliance probability (`personal_score = |personal_effect| x
compliance_probability`) as two deliberately separate quantities.
Personal effect models are consumed directly from Phase 12's own
regression output (never refit). A lightweight contextual bandit with
Thompson sampling (Normal-Normal conjugate update) governs the
`adaptive_policy` phase, restricted to the approved behavioral
catalogue, with an exploration-dominance safeguard preventing a
clearly superior intervention from ever being displaced by a lucky
sample from an unproven alternative. Every evidence query is strictly
`as_of_date`-scoped to prevent temporal leakage -- a recommendation for
day N can only see evidence from before day N.

## 18. Safety Layer

The Phase 14 safety and confidence layer sits above the completed
inference pipeline and decides only **whether/how** to show what was
already computed -- it never recomputes or modifies a DLMO prediction,
alignment score, or personalization state. Confidence gating is
consumed, never recomputed, from Phase 7's own fusion confidence
(thresholds 80/60/40, mapping to HIGH/MODERATE/LOW/NO_PREDICTION).
Four persistent unusual-pattern detectors operate over a rolling
14-day horizon, requiring at least 10 available observations each:
`persistent_late_phase`, `persistent_early_phase`,
`continuous_dlmo_drift`, and `low_alignment_despite_compliance` --
explicitly screening/escalation logic, never diagnosis of a named
condition. Only `continuous_dlmo_drift` is HIGH severity (which
suppresses the primary recommendation entirely); every other flag is
MODERATE (recommendation remains available alongside the flag). A flag
lifecycle (`DETECTED -> PERSISTING -> RESOLVED`) requires 7 consecutive
normal observations to resolve. A hard-coded catalogue/language guard
blocks any recommendation outside the approved behavioral categories
or containing medical/dosing language, and a defensive phrase scan
blocks specific prohibited statements. Every prediction-capable output
carries a fixed boundary note: *"Estimated from behavioral patterns.
Not a clinical measurement."*

## 19. Database Architecture

CircadianIQ's schema (14 tables, SQLAlchemy ORM, Alembic-migrated,
portable between SQLite and PostgreSQL) separates participant identity
(`participants`) from current mutable state (`participant_profiles`,
`personal_effect_models`, `bandit_state`) from immutable per-event
history (`daily_observations`, `physiology_logs`, `circadian_labels`,
`alignment_results`, `contributor_results`, `intervention_log`,
`intervention_effect_estimates`, `missing_data_events`,
`safety_flags`). Predictions use an event-sourced pattern: each
inference run inserts a new `circadian_labels` row rather than
overwriting the prior one, with an `is_latest` boolean pointer
identifying the currently-authoritative row while preserving full
audit history. Physiological signals (`physiology_logs`) are stored in
a structurally separate table specifically so Version-1 behavioral
code cannot "accidentally" consume them. See §22 for the three
documented limitations affecting this schema's provenance and
uniqueness guarantees, and `docs/architecture_diagrams.md` for an
entity-relationship diagram.

## 20. API/Application Architecture

The application layer (FastAPI, versioned under `/api/v1`) is
strictly layered: routes translate HTTP to typed Pydantic
request/response contracts only, application services orchestrate
calls into the existing Phase 4-14 domain modules (no biological, ML,
or safety logic lives in a route or service), and repositories are the
sole issuers of SQLAlchemy queries. GET endpoints are pure database
reads and never trigger computation; `POST
/participants/{id}/inference` is the only endpoint that computes
anything, calling the Phase 11 daily-inference orchestrator followed
by the Phase 14 safety evaluation in sequence. Idempotency passes
straight through from the orchestrator: a repeated inference call for
an already-computed participant/date returns the existing result
rather than duplicating it. **This is an explicitly documented
prototype**: it implements no authentication or authorization, no
production rate limiting, and no CORS/TLS configuration -- a
local-development research tool, not a production-secured service.

## 21. User Interface

The dashboard is a standalone React 19 + TypeScript (Vite) single-page
application that consumes the API exclusively over HTTP and contains
no domain, ML, statistical, or safety computation of its own -- every
displayed value is either returned verbatim by the API or drawn from a
small, centralized, pre-approved terminology table keyed off an
API-provided enum. It presents an estimated-biological-clock card with
a midnight-safe circular SVG visualization, an alignment score with a
5-component breakdown, a confidence badge with four states (high /
moderate / low / no-prediction), top contributors in strictly
non-causal language, a primary recommendation with up to two
supporting nudges, intervention progress, and a 7/30/90-day trajectory
chart whose clock-time series is unwrapped (not recomputed) for
continuous midnight-crossing rendering. Full safety UI (none / a
neutral "Pattern note" / a suppressed-recommendation high-severity
message) is driven entirely by API-provided safety state. A six-step
onboarding flow and a daily behavioral-entry form provide the only way
to add real (non-synthetic) participant history through the UI.

## 22. Limitations

This system carries limitations at every layer, most already noted
in-line above; the following are the limitations most material to
interpreting the system as a whole, plus the three specific gaps
identified and deliberately left unresolved by the Phase 17
system-wide adversarial audit (final status: **CONDITIONAL PASS**):

- **All validation is against synthetic data.** No CircadianIQ
  component -- feature engineering, the XGBoost model, the state
  engine, fusion, alignment, contributor ranking, recommendation
  selection, effectiveness estimation, or personalization -- has been
  validated against real human circadian/DLMO measurements. Strong
  performance on the synthetic held-out test set partly reflects the
  synthetic generator and the model sharing a common synthetic world,
  not independent validation against real biology.
- **No DB-level uniqueness constraint on `circadian_labels.is_latest`.**
  The single-current-row-per-participant-per-date invariant is
  enforced only by application discipline (the pipeline's own
  supersede-then-insert logic), not by a database constraint (e.g. a
  partial unique index). No observed incorrect output resulted from
  this during the Phase 17 audit, but it remains an unresolved
  schema-level gap.
- **Structured version-provenance columns are incomplete.** Of the
  full set of config/model versions that materially affect a given
  prediction (pipeline, feature-manifest, model, state, fusion,
  alignment, contributor, and recommendation config versions), only
  the XGBoost model version is a directly queryable column
  (`circadian_labels.training_run_id`). Most other versions are
  recorded only inside a `diagnostics`/`component_snapshot` JSON blob
  attached to the in-memory result, or not persisted at all --
  reconstructing "exactly which configuration produced this historical
  row" is not uniformly possible via a simple SQL query today.
- **`latest_recommendation_id_for_type` (the personalization catalogue
  lookup) is not `as_of_date`-scoped.** This is a catalogue-versioning
  gap -- if the recommendation catalogue itself changes over time, a
  personalization query issued "as of" a past date could see a later
  catalogue state -- and is explicitly **not** a participant-data leak:
  a dedicated temporal-replay proof in the Phase 17 audit confirmed
  that participant behavioral data is correctly `as_of_date`-scoped
  everywhere it is read.
- **Every locked weight, threshold, and decay constant throughout the
  system** (fusion base weights, alignment's 60/40 split and five
  behavioral weights, contributor's 60/40 split, recommendation
  severity bands and graduated-target fraction, safety's four flag
  thresholds, personalization's graduation thresholds) is a documented
  project design choice or engineering parameter, not an
  independently, statistically, or clinically validated constant. See
  `docs/system_validation_and_audit.md` for the complete, file-cited
  inventory of which numeric assumptions are literature-backed versus
  project-modeling-assumptions versus engineering choices.
- **No statistical significance testing is performed anywhere in the
  codebase.** Model evaluation reports point-estimate metrics (MAE,
  RMSE, percentile-within-band) and cross-validation fold spread
  (mean ± standard deviation across 5 folds); no p-values, confidence
  intervals over the *held-out test* metrics, or formal hypothesis
  tests are computed for the model comparison. (The Phase 12
  effectiveness regression does compute OLS standard errors and a
  confidence interval for its own personal-effect coefficient, which
  is a separate, narrower estimator, not a claim of model-comparison
  significance.)
- **A `feature_manifest_version` labeling inconsistency exists between
  the trained model artifact's own metadata and the current code.**
  `models/model_metadata_v1.json` records `"feature_manifest_version":
  "phase4-v1"` (hard-coded at the point the training pipeline persists
  a training run), while the current feature manifest module
  (`app.features.feature_manifest.FEATURE_MANIFEST_VERSION`) reports
  `"1.0.0"`. This is a naming/versioning inconsistency between two
  labels that both refer to the same, unchanged 68-feature manifest --
  not evidence that the underlying feature set changed between
  training and the current codebase.
- **The API and UI are prototypes.** No authentication, authorization,
  production rate limiting, or CORS/TLS configuration exists; this is
  an explicit, documented development-only assumption.
- **No completeness-degraded evaluation exists.** Every window in the
  Phase 3 synthetic dataset has `completeness == 1.0` (no missing
  values were ever injected), so the model's/alignment's/contributor
  engine's behavior under real-world missingness is implemented and
  unit-tested, but not evaluated end-to-end against a realistically
  incomplete dataset.
- **Performance measurement was not load/production-scale tested.**
  Phase 17 measured wall-clock inference time at n=1/10/100
  participants on one development machine; no load testing or
  optimization was performed.

## 23. Future Work

- Replace or supplement the synthetic-data validation with real,
  IRB-approved human behavioral and DLMO measurement data, which would
  be the necessary next step before any clinical-relevance claim could
  be considered.
- Add a partial unique index (or equivalent DB-level constraint) on
  `circadian_labels` enforcing at most one `is_latest=True` row per
  `(participant_id, target_date)`, closing the first documented Phase
  17 gap at the schema level rather than relying solely on application
  discipline.
- Add structured, directly queryable provenance columns for the
  remaining config versions (state/fusion/alignment/contributor/
  recommendation) currently reconstructable only from JSON diagnostics,
  closing the second documented Phase 17 gap.
- Make `latest_recommendation_id_for_type` (and any other
  catalogue-lookup helper used by personalization) explicitly
  `as_of_date`-scoped against catalogue effective-dating, closing the
  third documented Phase 17 gap.
- Reconcile the `feature_manifest_version` label between
  `app.features.feature_manifest.FEATURE_MANIFEST_VERSION` and the
  value `app.models.training` writes into persisted model metadata, so
  both consistently describe the same manifest under one version
  string.
- Seed the optional `PERSONALIZATION_MATURE` demo scenario (requiring
  roughly 12 weeks / 42+ observations of simulated intervention-logging
  and compliance-outcome cycles) once a maturity-focused demonstration
  is prioritized -- not included in this release's demo database (see
  `docs/demo_runbook.md` and `release/demo/demo_scenarios.json`).
- Introduce a deliberately-degraded (missing-data-injected) synthetic
  dataset variant to evaluate model/alignment/contributor robustness
  under realistic missingness, rather than relying solely on unit-level
  fallback-chain tests.
- Add authentication/authorization, production rate limiting, and
  CORS/TLS configuration before any deployment beyond local research
  use, per the documented prototype security limitations in
  `docs/api_architecture.md`.
- Perform formal load/production-scale performance testing beyond the
  n=1/10/100 single-machine measurement already recorded in
  `docs/system_validation_and_audit.md`.
- Evaluate hyperparameter tuning for the XGBoost model (none was
  performed in this release) and re-evaluate whether XGBoost's native
  multi-output regression path offers meaningful efficiency gains over
  the current two-independent-model architecture without losing
  per-target interpretability.
