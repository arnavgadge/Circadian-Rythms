# Introduction (Draft)

**Status: draft.** This document is a methodology/results draft toward an eventual IEEE-style paper; it is not a finished or submitted manuscript.

**Release version:** v1.0.0-research-demo. **Phase 17 audit status:** CONDITIONAL PASS.

---

## 1. Motivation

Circadian phase — most precisely operationalized in chronobiology as Dim Light Melatonin Onset (DLMO), the clock time at which endogenous melatonin secretion begins under dim-light laboratory conditions — is a biological marker with substantial relevance to sleep timing, alertness, and behavioral scheduling. Direct laboratory DLMO measurement, however, requires repeated timed saliva or plasma sampling under controlled dim-light conditions, and is consequently expensive, intrusive, and impractical outside of specialized research or clinical settings. This creates a general and long-standing interest, across the circadian-science and behavioral-computing communities, in whether circadian phase can instead be *estimated* from more routinely observable signals — sleep timing, light exposure, screen use, meal timing, exercise, and caffeine intake — collected passively or with low participant burden.

Prior work in circadian phase estimation from behavioral and wearable proxies has explored various approaches to this general estimation problem (citations to be added in a future revision); this draft does not attempt to survey or cite that literature, and no specific prior study, author, or result is referenced here. We instead motivate the problem generically and describe our own system's design and synthetic-data evaluation.

## 2. Problem Framing

We treat behaviorally-derived circadian phase estimation strictly as an *estimation* problem: producing a computed estimate from behavioral and environmental observations, never a claim of equivalence to, or substitute for, an actual clinical DLMO measurement. A useful estimation system, in this framing, must do more than produce a single number — it must also (a) express how confident it is in that estimate, (b) translate the estimate into something actionable for a participant's own routine, (c) avoid overreaching into medical or diagnostic claims, and (d) be evaluable end-to-end, not only at the level of an isolated prediction model.

## 3. Contribution

This draft documents CircadianIQ, an end-to-end synthetic-data research architecture spanning: a biologically-motivated synthetic data generator producing paired behavioral/DLMO trajectories; a leakage-controlled feature engineering layer; a machine-learned phase estimator (XGBoost) fused with an independent mechanistic state-based estimator; an alignment-scoring layer relating the estimate to a participant's own behavior; a deterministic, catalogue-driven recommendation engine; an observational intervention-effectiveness and personalization system; and a safety/confidence layer that gates what is shown without altering any underlying computation. The system was developed across a sequence of phases culminating in a system-wide adversarial audit (Phase 17), which concluded a status of **CONDITIONAL PASS** — found and fixed six real defects, and left three specific limitations deliberately unresolved and documented (see `limitations_draft.md`).

The contribution of this work is the architecture and its synthetic-data validation methodology, not a claim of real-world circadian-estimation accuracy. Because the entire training and evaluation pipeline operates on data generated by the project's own synthetic model, strong performance against synthetic ground truth demonstrates that the estimation and fusion mechanics behave as designed — it is not, and must not be read as, independent evidence of accuracy against real human physiology.

## 4. Scope and Non-Claims

To avoid any ambiguity, this draft explicitly does **not** claim: that CircadianIQ has been validated against real human DLMO measurements; that any reported metric describes real-world or clinical accuracy; that the system is a finished, production-ready, or clinically usable product; that any locked weight, threshold, or catalogue value used throughout the pipeline is an independently validated physiological or clinical constant (each is a documented project design choice); or that formal statistical significance testing was performed on the headline model-comparison results (none was). What this draft does claim is narrower: an end-to-end system was built, its components integrate correctly, and its synthetic-data behavior was measured and audited as described in the sections that follow.
